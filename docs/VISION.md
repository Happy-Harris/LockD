Yes. Here’s the throw-shit-at-the-wall board. Some are obvious, some are deranged, some might become the reason people care about Lock’d.

1. Ghost Session — overlay today against the last comparable workout set-by-set: “last time 80×8, today 80×9.” You’re literally racing your previous self.
2. Plateau Autopsy — when a lift stalls, Lock’d explains the pattern: missed reps, flat e1RM, falling volume, rising RPE, inconsistent frequency, etc.
3. PR Near-Miss Detector — “one more rep would’ve tied your 8RM” / “+2.5 kg would’ve been a new 5RM.”
4. Lift DNA — each exercise develops a profile: strongest rep range, typical progression rate, best-performing days, usual RPE drift, average exposures before a jump.
5. Momentum Score — not a fake 0–100 wellness number; more like “3 of your 5 tracked lifts are moving upward over the last 4 exposures.”
6. Progression Personality — “this lift responds better to rep progression than load jumps” based on your own history.
7. Set Quality History — not judging form, just tracking whether sets are becoming heavier, cleaner, lower-RPE, or more repeatable.
8. Workout Diff — after finishing, show exactly what changed from last time: +2 reps, +5 kg, -1 set, RPE -1.
9. Session Replay — timeline of the workout: exercise order, set completions, rests, PRs, edits, pauses. Like a match replay for training.
10. Training Chronicle — eras, layoffs, comebacks, blocks, strongest periods, “the summer you lived on incline bench,” all auto-detected from history.
11. Yearly Receipt — total sessions, top lifts, biggest jumps, most-used exercises, longest streak, comeback of the year, first/last PR.
12. Milestone Posters — 100 kg bench, first bodyweight pull-up, 20 pull-ups, 200 kg deadlift, 1,000 completed working sets.
13. Lift Family Tree — show how one movement evolved: machine press → dumbbell press → bench → incline emphasis → etc.
14. Exercise Retirement — archive a movement and Lock’d gives you its career stats before it disappears from active use.
15. Comeback Detection — after a long gap: “Back after 43 days. Last recorded bench: 90×6.” Then tracks the rebuild.
16. Peak Finder — identify your strongest 4–8 week period for each lift or muscle group.
17. Training Eras you can name — auto-detect first, then let the user rename: “Winter Bulk,” “Ramadan 2027,” “Shoulder rehab,” “Unemployed & Dangerous.”
18. Local Video Set Vault — attach a clip to a set, stored locally. Compare today’s squat with six months ago side-by-side.
19. Technique Timeline — scrub through saved clips for one lift over time. No AI required to make this cool.
20. Machine Memory — save seat height, lever position, handle setting, pin quirks, and “this gym’s stack lies.” Next time the machine appears, the setup appears.
21. Gym Equipment Profiles — “Hammer Strength incline at Gym A ≠ Gym B.” Same movement family, separate machine history if desired.
22. Smart Exercise Swap — “cable row occupied?” Show substitutes matching muscle, movement pattern, tracking style, and equipment available.
23. Substitution Memory — remembers what you normally swap in when something’s unavailable.
24. Routine Mutation Capture — if you keep changing the same Routine during workouts, Lock’d notices: “You’ve replaced leg press with hack squat 4 times. Update the Routine?”
25. Program Compiler — user defines simple progression rules like “8–12 reps; once all sets hit 12, add 2.5 kg.” Lock’d executes the rule automatically.
26. Progression Sandbox — preview what the next 6 sessions would look like under double progression, linear loading, top-set/backoff, etc.
27. Block Builder — not just Routines: 4–8 week blocks with progression rules, planned exercise changes, deloads, and target rep zones.
28. Auto-Deload Call — deterministic evidence: repeated stalls + elevated RPE + falling reps + high recent workload. Shows the evidence, doesn’t pretend it can read your soul.
29. Volume Response History — “your chest performance usually improved during weeks with 10–14 credited sets.” Personal descriptive history, not fake causal science.
30. Rest-Time Intelligence — “your heavy bench sets perform better after 3+ min than after <2 min.” Based on your own logs.
31. RPE Calibration — compare reported RPE with what happened next. “Your RPE 8 sets usually still have 2–3 reps of progression headroom.”
32. Left/Right Drift — unilateral trend view that flags when one side keeps falling behind in reps/load.
33. Rep-Speed Proxy without sensors — just a manual “easy / normal / grind” tap after a set. Over time, progress isn’t only weight.
34. Strength Map — visual body map showing where your actual progression is occurring, separate from where your training volume is going.
35. Goal Lens Packs — Powerbuilding, Hypertrophy, Strength, Calisthenics, Hybrid, General. Same data, radically different default home/analytics emphasis.
36. Powerbuilding Lens — pins 3 goal lifts, rep PRs, e1RM trend, muscle-set coverage, and next targets.
37. Hypertrophy Lens — progression hit rate, hard sets by muscle, RIR/RPE trend, exercise rotation, muscle-specific history.
38. Calisthenics Lens — progression chains, weighted bodyweight, assistance reduction, hold duration, harder-variation milestones.
39. Hybrid Lens — strength + conditioning blocks, rounds, distance, duration, session load, while keeping tonnage honest.
40. Exercise Progression Chains — knee push-up → push-up → weighted push-up → archer; assisted pull-up → pull-up → weighted pull-up.
41. Unlockable Variations — not gamified nonsense; when performance reaches a user-defined threshold, harder variants become suggested options.
42. Weighted Bodyweight Done Properly — body mass + external load displayed separately; no pretending bodyweight tonnage is equivalent to barbell tonnage.
43. Assistance Progression — for pull-ups/dips/machines: progress is less assistance, which most apps represent badly.
44. Conditioning Blocks — rounds, work/rest intervals, distance, duration, carries, bag rounds, sleds, assault-bike intervals.
45. Custom Field Exercises — build an exercise from fields: distance + load, rounds + time, reps + assistance, duration + RPE.
46. “Beat This” Mode — pick an old session and explicitly challenge it today.
47. PR Hunt Mode — select a lift and a record target. Lock’d structures the workout screen around the attempt without becoming a program generator.
48. Warm-Up Auto-Pilot — once working weight is selected, generated warm-ups appear inline and adapt to the available plates.
49. Rest Timer Personalities — heavy compound / hypertrophy / isolation timer presets, or learned per exercise from your history.
50. Live Activity / Lock-Screen Rest Timer — native Capacitor build shows remaining rest without reopening Lock’d.
51. Watch Companion — complete set, view next target, timer controls, current exercise. Phone stays on the bench.
52. NFC Gym Tags — tap an NFC sticker on a machine to open that exercise and its last numbers. Completely unnecessary. Also extremely cool.
53. QR Routine Sharing — routine/program becomes a local file encoded or linked through QR. No account required.
54. Workout File Format — .lockd files for routines, programs, training blocks, even complete exported sessions.
55. Creator Packs without a marketplace — download a signed/open .lockd program file from anywhere, import locally, creator attribution included.
56. Program Forking — import somebody’s program, modify it, and retain “based on X” lineage locally.
57. Session Notes that become Knowledge — exercise note like “seat 4, handles neutral” automatically appears next time that exercise comes up.
58. Pin a Lesson — after a workout, save one sentence to the exercise or Routine: “70 kg was too aggressive after 3×10.”
59. Training Search — natural-ish local search without an LLM: “bench workouts above 90 kg,” “leg days under 45 minutes,” “sessions where squat RPE was 9+.”
60. Ask the Lab — eventually: “Why does my bench keep stalling at 90?” It answers from your data, cites the exact sessions/sets, then separates personal evidence from research evidence.
61. What Changed? — Ask the Lab-style deterministic card: “Your last four sessions added reps but not load; average RPE rose from 7.8 to 9.0.”
62. Counterfactual Viewer — “If this set had been 2 reps higher, would it have been a PR?” Tiny feature. Weirdly addictive.
63. Training Forecast — not “you’ll bench 140 by December.” More honest: “At your recent progression rate, the next likely milestone is 100×8.”
64. Milestone Queue — upcoming reachable records: “+1 rep to match 10RM,” “+2.5 kg to beat 5RM.”
65. Personal Record Taxonomy — not just heaviest weight: heaviest 1–12RM, best e1RM, most reps at a load, longest hold, least assistance, fastest distance.
66. Workout Identity Cards — “Pull day · 47 min · 16 working sets · 2 records.” Shareable, clean, not social-feed sludge.
67. PR Moment Themes — serious receipt, brutalist plate card, old-school meet card, minimal black card, “loud mode.”
68. Training Lore — optional titles generated deterministically from history: “100 kg club,” “20-rep pull-up,” “500 sessions logged,” “The Comeback.”
69. Named PRs — user can mark one PR as meaningful and annotate why: “first 100 kg after surgery,” “dad was there,” whatever matters.
70. Memory Pins — attach a photo/note locally to a workout. Years later the training log becomes an actual life archive.
71. Workout Soundtrack Bookmark — optionally save what you were listening to as plain text or local metadata. Completely frivolous. I still like it.
72. Moodless Recovery — instead of asking “how do you feel?”, optionally import sleep/resting HR/HRV locally and show context next to performance.
73. Recovery Overlay — “best deadlift performances happened after ≥7h sleep” without claiming causation.
74. Bodyweight Context — strength changes alongside bodyweight: absolute and relative progress side-by-side, only when the user opts in.
75. Mass Phase Detection — if bodyweight trends strongly up/down, annotate strength charts with that period instead of pretending all PRs occurred under equal conditions.
76. Training Density — work completed per minute, but separated by exercise type so it doesn’t reward rushing heavy compounds.
77. Rest Discipline View — show actual rest distributions by exercise. Maybe you think you rest 2 minutes. Turns out it’s 4:37 because TikTok got you.
78. The “Bullshit Detector” — catches impossible or suspicious logs: accidental 1,000 kg, 0-second sets, duplicate completions. Lets you fix them before they poison analytics.
79. History Integrity Ledger — edits to completed workouts can optionally show “edited after completion” and preserve the original values locally.
80. Receipt Hash — nerd mode: generate a local checksum for a completed workout so exported records can prove they weren’t modified afterward.
